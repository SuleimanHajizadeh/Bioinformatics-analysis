## Summary of the Study

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains one of the most challenging subtypes to manage clinically due to the absence of targetable hormone receptors and HER2 amplification. In this study, I performed a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis of RNA-sequencing data from 20 human subjects (10 TNBC biopsies vs. 10 normal adjacent tissues; GEO: GSE58135) using a fully reproducible bioinformatics pipeline.

**Key findings include:**

1. **450 significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs)** were identified (FDR < 0.05, |Log2FC| > 2.0), with HISAT2 achieving a 94.8% read mapping rate.
2. **PI3K-Akt signaling hyperactivation** was the dominant enriched pathway in KEGG analysis, consistent with a known oncogenic driver in solid tumors.
3. **Heterogeneous immune infiltration patterns** were identified: a subset of tumors co-expressed CXCL13 with immune checkpoints (PDCD1, CTLA4), suggesting a transcriptomic signature that may predict responsiveness to checkpoint blockade therapies.
4. **Tumor microenvironment (TME) deconvolution** revealed a dominance of immunosuppressive M2 macrophages in aggressive TNBC phenotypes.

## Significance and Originality

This work directly addresses the challenge of patient stratification in TNBC immunotherapy, a significant unmet clinical need. All data used in this study are publicly available through NCBI (GEO: GSE58135; SRA: SRP042620), ensuring full reproducibility. The analysis was conducted using established, open-source bioinformatics tools (DESeq2, clusterProfiler, pheatmap) in R and Python.
