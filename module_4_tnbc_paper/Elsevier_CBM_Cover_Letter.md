**Title:** *"Transcriptomic Characterization of Primary Breast Carcinoma Identifies Distinct Immune Infiltration Patterns and PI3K-Akt Hyperactivation"*

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript titled *"Transcriptomic Characterization of Primary Breast Carcinoma Identifies Distinct Immune Infiltration Patterns and PI3K-Akt Hyperactivation"* for consideration as an original Research Article in *Computers in Biology and Medicine*. I believe the mission of *Computers in Biology and Medicine*—to publish significant advances at the intersection of computer science, algorithmic methodologies, and biomedicine—perfectly aligns with the machine learning and spatial deconvolution frameworks utilized in my study.

Despite massive clinical efforts, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) continuously eludes broad-spectrum therapeutic success due to its extreme biological heterogeneity. Leveraging a rigorous bioinformatic systems biology approach, I decoded the core transcriptomic topography of 20 human primary breast carcinoma and adjacent normal biopsies. My analysis bypassed traditional limits of bulk profiling through an established machine learning and spatial deconvolution pipeline, yielding the following mechanistic discoveries:

1. **Topology of the Malignant Transcriptome:** I successfully resolved the profound baseline divergence between malignant and normal tissues (capturing 450 robust differentially expressed genes, AUC=1.0) and definitively isolated the PI3K-Akt signaling cascade as a generalized master regulatory network driving the TNBC phenotype.
2. **Quantitative TME Deconvolution:** I mathematically deconvoluted the complex tumor microenvironment (TME), mapping the aggressive M2 Macrophage dominance against the lack of cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell infiltration. 
3. **Identification of Checkpoint Priming:** Crucially, I discovered an internal phenotypic subset within the TNBC cohort defined by the robust co-expression of the chemokine *CXCL13* and the immune checkpoint receptors *PDCD1* and *CTLA4*. This transcriptomic signature effectively stratifies patients into "immune-hot" and "immune-cold" factions, carrying direct translational potential for guiding targeted PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint blockade therapies.

My findings mechanistically underline the critical necessity for single-patient stratification over generalized breast cancer protocols. In my Discussion, I also present an unvarnished and fully transparent assessment of methodological boundaries, explicitly pointing to sample size constraints and the necessity for prospective large-cohort computational validation. I am confident that this analytical rigor conforms strictly to the exacting mathematical and transparency standards required by Elsevier.

This manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not currently under consideration by any other journal. As the sole author, I have approved the final version and agree with its submission to *Computers in Biology and Medicine*. I declare no competing financial or academic interests.
